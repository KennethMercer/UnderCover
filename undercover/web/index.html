Advanced settings
Voice octave

Moves only the new voice by whole octaves, so it stays in key. Female singer → male voice: try −1. Male singer → female voice: try +1.

−6−30+3+6

Transposes the whole song, music included, to suit the voice's range. ±1 to ±3 sounds most natural.

Higher sounds more like the target voice, but can blur words. Has no effect if the voice has no index.

Keeps breaths and "s"/"t" sounds natural. Slide right to turn it off.

original singer'snew voice's own

Whose loudness rises and falls the new vocal follows.

Separation quality

How carefully vocals are pulled out of the song. Each level is cached separately.

Backing voice octave

A second voice sings the backing vocals. It follows one note at a time, so big stacked harmonies come out thinner.

Reverb on the new vocal

Auto puts back the amount "Remove reverb first" took out, and adds none otherwise.

Export format

Pulls the singing toward the notes of the song's key. A little (0.3–0.5) tidies wobbly notes; full is the robotic autotune sound.

Train a voice

Name a person and UnderCover finds clips of them, keeps only their voice, and trains a voice model you can use for covers. For personal use: don't pass the results off as the real person.

1 · Who should it sound like?

Clips are kept in cache/training

Searches YouTube for interviews, speeches and podcasts (or live and isolated vocals for singing). Untick anything that looks wrong.

Prepares the ticked sources (and more if there isn't 10 min of voice yet), confirms the main voice, trains (~40 min) and adds it to your voices. You can still step in at any point.

2 · Is this the right voice?

The voice heard most across your sources. Every phrase is compared with it, so other people (interviewers, the crowd) get left out.
